## Objective and acceptance

Use the six supplied interior references to guide room identity and subsequent art. Owner prefers wall-length assets because they give rooms character. This establishes a design direction, not approval of a newly generated asset or an instruction to rebuild every existing room.

## Accepted decisions and constraints

Wall installations should feel like connected, purpose-built interiors: adjoining cabinets, operating consoles, equipment bays and work surfaces with shared backing, plinths and service runs. Length comes from multiple human-scale functional bays, not enlarged machinery. Reserve clear floor for crew circulation and a meaningful focal object where appropriate.

Translate the references into the existing top-down game camera. Controls face the room interior; north/south/east/west variants need corresponding working faces. Keep wall contact, low hull geometry and doorway gaps authoritative. The source photography's perspective and ceilings are not room geometry templates.

Maintain the current matte-material rule: powder-coated metal, molded panels, rubber, fabric, quiet glass and restrained pixel highlights. Preserve department identity. Selected science/medical and crew areas can use warm cream, gray-green or olive; engineering can retain industrial steel and orange. Do not make every department either ivory or blue-black.

## Production interpretation

Begin with the activity and a sequence of functional bays. Use an asymmetric mix of screen sizes, switch clusters, vents, storage and workspace within a coherent cabinet system. Avoid repeating the same symmetric cartridge-bank-plus-keyboard pair across unrelated rooms. Shared construction is useful; repeated subject matter weakens identity.

Favor tactile retro-industrial interfaces: chunky recessed screen bezels, keys, toggle groups, rotary selectors, speaker grilles and occasional supported handsets. Cyan/green phosphor screens and sparse amber/red status lamps are accents. The very bright indicator-wall reference supplies density and organization, not a new brightness target. Keep lighting localized and operational animation separate from static art.

At game scale, larger silhouettes and functional clusters carry detail; individual tiny switches do not all need bright pixels. Review the candidate in its room beside crew, with controls accessible and wall contact visible. Include subdued practical lighting and occasional work traces such as a stored headset, clipboard or mug where the room supports them.

Promising applications: a shared communications/control wall, an instrument-lined laboratory bench with a central specimen task, and a warm crew mess with fitted storage and a continuous kitchenette. These are directions to choose from, not newly approved room definitions or gameplay mechanics.
