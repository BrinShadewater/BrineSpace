**Restore a silent underwater station into a place people can inhabit, while
something about it remains wrong.**

BrineSpace combines industrial retro-futurism, practical pressure-hull construction,
biopunk science and restrained cosmic unease. Heavy equipment, useful furniture,
living plants and personal belongings make the station credible. BRINE has kept a
small part of it alive. Restoration brings back function, human routines and warmth.

The emotional range is quiet industry, isolation, fragile comfort, curiosity and
unease. Horror is suggested through context and exceptional disturbances. Everyday
labs are not horror sets, and living quarters are not uniformly decayed.

The setting is an ocean planet's underwater station. Older orbital fiction and
space vistas are historical. Existing room names are not authority to add stars,
spaceship launch bays or orbital scenery. Do not rename rooms or change mechanics
as part of a style task.

One playable floor shares a consistent connection plane. Pylons, foundations,
seabed, water and excavated rock imply depth around it; they do not introduce stacked
buildable floors. Keep exterior scenery compatible with actual terrain and access.

## 2. The visual signature

Companion implementation note, September26: Margot's accepted first polish and

tracks; River keeps ivory/sage panels, amber lens and wheels. The new robot pack is

**Detailed hand-painted industrial sprites, with muted local colour, substantial
construction, restrained diffuse shading and readable silhouettes.**

- Paint the object through its construction: panel seams, vents, fasteners, fabric
  seams, wood grain, fitted handles, joints and serviceable components.
- Use varied but controlled tones within each material. Matte does not mean flat
  colour, lost depth or a uniformly darkened image.
- Use edge definition and contact shading to separate parts. Avoid thick uniform
  cartoon outlines, black blobs and disconnected fragments.
- Preserve a rich painted surface at master size and a clear functional shape at
  gameplay size. Fine texture supports the shape; it must not become noisy grit.
- Do not convert this prop style to coarse pixel art, smooth vector illustration,
  photorealistic rendering, glossy 3D product imagery or chibi proportions.
  Existing character/environment assets retain their own accepted source identities;
  this is not authorization to repaint them.

The accepted images are the measuring stick. Older references to pixel-art packs,
universal ivory laboratories, aggressive brightening curves or mandatory wear do
not define this prop standard.

## 4. Camera, scale and composition

Match the supplied accepted prop's overhead/cutaway view, visible top surfaces,
shallow front depth and facing. There is no newly specified numeric camera angle.
Do not turn furniture into tall frontal elevations or machinery into isometric
showpieces. Floor hatches and pads remain floor-plane objects.

Current station props v2 retain one painted facing while positions change with room
rotation. The older instruction to generate inward-facing art on every wall does
not authorize rotating, mirroring or replacing this accepted family. For integration,
follow [station props v2](STATION_PROPS_V2_2026-09-25.md) and current renderer behavior.
Any future camera/facing conversion is an explicit design change.

Compare equipment against the established crew at actual gameplay size. Beds must
hold bodies; seats, controls and tables must be usable. Do not shrink furniture
until a collision check passes. A master image's resolution is not a world scale.

Compose around a recognizable activity: operating, treating, storing, eating,
resting, cultivating or maintaining. Substantial equipment and its useful supports
form work areas. Small accessories add lived experience, not filler. Preserve
clear operator positions, door approaches and intentional empty floor.

Visual overlap is valid when it describes physical support or a deliberate assembly.
Collision, draw order and interaction anchors are separate checks. Owner-authored
Studio layouts are protected; this bible does not request a furnishing rollout.

## 5. Colour and departments

Use neutral structural materials with muted department accents. Colour identifies
function without becoming a full-image wash. Cream stays cream, blue stays blue,
wood stays warm; do not solve consistency by globally darkening or desaturating.

The hex values below are **interface identity colours**, not mandatory paint samples
or brightness targets for sprites. Match painted shades to the accepted references.

Corridors use neutral grey (#9aa2a8 in current room overrides). Derelict is a
condition/registry category, not an eighth peer department. Do not revive the old
separate Medical, Logistics or Data department palette tables.

A hybrid has one primary identity and localized secondary equipment. Do not average
several departments into a new palette. Unusual specimens can be coloured without
glowing. A colour accent alone is not a new room design.

BRINE's established pearl-white aquatic core remains a special focal identity:
quiet, precise, serene and uncanny. It does not make all Robotics props pearl-white.
Use `RoomDatabase.room_color(id)` for interface room colours and respect current
overrides; visual prose is not authority to add a new runtime override.

## 6. Materials, wear and light

| Material | Required reading |
|---|---|
| Painted metal/composite | Matte colour mass, diffuse volume, fitted seams, restrained wear |
| Dull steel and pipework | Subtle midtone variation; no chrome strips or white glint chains |
| Brass/copper | Muted warm material, shaped joints; no polished jewellery finish |
| Wood | Warm grain and constructed rails/posts; no rustic rot by default |
| Fabric | Seams and restrained weave, soft volume, complete cushions and upholstery |
| Glass/vessels | Readable contents and volume; avoid broad white reflection bands and diagonal shine |
| Screens | Near-black inactive surfaces; no decorative glossy streaks or baked luminous display |
| Plants/biological matter | Legible leaf/culture shapes and natural variation; ordinary growth is not contamination |

Default restored interiors are maintained with light use. Prefer slightly less grime,
especially on scientific/high-tech equipment. Scuffs belong near handling points.
Avoid blanket rust, dense scratching, heavy mottling or random chipped edges.

New, operational, used, damaged, derelict and contaminated are distinct conditions.
Heavy decay, leaks or intrusion require a deliberate condition brief and must not
silently migrate into the clean master family. Keep department identity visible
beneath damage where plausible.

Base exports have no baked glow, sparks, steam, flowing-water effect or exterior cast
shadow added for drama. Internal contact/occlusion shading preserves form. Static
contents that define the accepted object stay intact. Functional lights, motion and
effects are separate states/layers when requested, bound to actual operation.
Do not repaint accepted objects merely to enforce a literal zero-highlight rule:
the goal is the restrained material reading of the accepted set.

The surrounding ocean can be dark blue-green, silted and partially concealed by
survey/fog state. Interior art keeps its local palette. Do not impose an underwater
blue filter on every room. Localized light gives information; it does not make
every pipe, specimen and screen compete for attention.

## 7. Complete objects and clean transparency

A cutout must contain the **entire object**, not merely a plausible silhouette.

- Rails run continuously behind foreground plants/books. Reconstruct hidden
  structure from the room reference when the source cut has erased it.
- Preserve cabinet backs, tabletop surfaces, front aprons, feet, supports, machine
  bases, vessel interiors and real floor-contact parts.
- Genuine openings between pipes, cables, legs and frames are transparent.
  Room floor trapped inside those openings must be removed.
- Never add a backing plate to conceal an extraction problem. Never erase a solid
  panel because it resembles the background.
- Preserve all pieces and their spacing in an accepted assembly. Do not split or
  merge furniture simply because its image contains multiple objects.
- Use genuine alpha, no painted checkerboard, dark haze, floor scraps or bright
  fringe. Inspect on light, dark and checker/contrasting backgrounds.
- Keep full silhouette and adequate canvas margin. Inspect both master and native
  export: resizing can hide a missing support or introduce a fringe.

The bookcase rail, couch support and coffee-table apron repairs are explicit
examples of structural completeness, not optional decorative polish.

## 8. Characters, interface and voice

Characters retain their established identities, proportions, clothing and equipment.
Match surrounding prop scale to them. Preserve coherent bodies and grounded contact
during movement; detailed still art does not establish a good animation.
Use the [character pipeline](../skills/brinespace-character-pipeline/SKILL.md) for
character work, with continuous native motion and transition review when relevant.

Interface typography uses bundled Barlow Semi Condensed; the station log remains
monospace. Department colour, icon and text work together. Keep status warnings
distinct from decorative paint. Fine text in prop art must not carry required
gameplay information; do not invent readable signage or labels during extraction.

BRINE is damaged, dry, opinionated and a little haunted. Never chirpy or a reassuring
assistant. Human warmth comes from inhabited spaces and the crew; BRINE's restraint
lets that warmth contrast with uncertainty.

> “I have mapped thirty-seven ways to run out of oxygen. I recommend none of them.”

Use concrete station observations, clipped wit and implications rather than constant
jokes, melodrama or exposition. This aesthetic does not change resources, failures,
discoveries or the current gameplay loop.

## 9. Reusable production brief

Fill this in before producing a new asset:

- **Identity/function:** prop ID, what it does, primary department, condition.
- **References:** exact locked IDs/files and each reference's role.
- **Geometry:** camera/facing, complete parts, actual support surfaces, genuine gaps.
- **Materials:** dominant surfaces, local colours, texture and wear level.
- **Scale:** intended native canvas, crew comparison, operator side and footprint.
- **State:** neutral base or explicitly requested active/damaged variant.
- **Output:** transparent master, native derivative and provenance; integration scope.

Prompt starting point:

> Create a BrineSpace [subject] in the supplied accepted reference style. Detailed
> hand-painted industrial retro-futurism for an underwater station. Match the
> reference camera, proportions and muted local palette. Matte painted metal,
> dull hardware, subtle diffuse shading, selective seams and material texture.
> Maintained with light wear, especially clean for scientific equipment. Preserve
> every structural part and all genuine openings. Complete isolated silhouette
> with true transparency. No added floor/backing, exterior shadow, chrome glints,
> glossy screen stripes, glow or animated effects. [Subject, condition, geometry
> and native-scale requirements.]

For repair, name the exact defect and preserve the remaining accepted design.
A prompt is a starting brief, not proof that an output meets the standard.
Use imagegen for image generation/repair in this established export workflow;
normal resizing, previews and file validation can use deterministic tools.
Keep raw outputs and record selected revisions. Do not use Higgsfield without an
explicit owner request.

## 10. Acceptance and maintaining the lock

Companion recovery preserves the same body artwork, palette and world scale inside
the container and after exit. Occupants are already behind closed panels and become
visible through their opening apertures; do not open an empty crate and then insert
the character. Keep hinges attached and chassis contact fixed, with intermediate
poses and a settled open endpoint. Doors, lid, rim and ramp occlude the appropriate
body regions. A held opening pose joins reboot without a colour, size or body pop.
See [rescue handoff](ROBOT_RESCUE_HANDOFF_2026-09-27.md) for the implementation.

1. Compare the candidate with the named locked references at matching display scale.
2. Check design/facing, complete parts, palette, material reading and condition.
3. Inspect alpha on contrasting backgrounds, including enclosed openings.
4. Review the original-size derivative for detail density and recognizability.
5. If installing, separately verify bindings, crew-relative size, floor contact,
   relevant orientations, routes, interactions and cards. File-size equality does
   not establish placement compatibility.
6. Record exact selected files/hashes and review scope. Preserve originals and
   owner data. Owner approval and runtime/package verification are separate facts.

For a new style direction, create a named candidate with explicit differences.
Update this bible and the reference lock only after owner selection. Until then,
it does not replace the current standard. A new family should be judged beside
several accepted functions before broad production.

Keep this document a specification. Put logs, rejected candidates, per-asset repairs,
test counts and pending implementation tasks in dated handoffs or ledgers. Link
accepted changes here without accumulating another chronological diary.

The clean-export set is the approved **visual reference family**, not a release or
integration certificate. Rights remain governed by [NOTICE.md](../NOTICE.md).
